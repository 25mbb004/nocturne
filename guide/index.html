THE MAKING OF

Nocturne

How an AI designed, built, and shipped a bioluminescent
mini-game — and how you can do the same.

01 — THE BRIEFOne game, maximum atmosphere

Nocturne was built end-to-end by Claude (Fable 5) inside Claude Code, working autonomously: concept, art direction, code, sound, iteration, and deployment. The brief was simple — build a mini-game that demonstrates taste — so every asset here is procedural. No stock images, no downloaded models, no sprite sheets. If you see it or hear it, it was generated by math at runtime.

The concept came first, because atmosphere needs a reason to exist: you are a wisp of bioluminescence in the deep ocean, ferrying light down to a reef that has gone dark. Gather twelve motes, carry them to a dormant coral cluster, and it blooms. Void urchins drift the water and knock the light out of you. Wake all seven clusters and the abyss remembers what the sun was.

02 — PALETTEAbyssal ink, four ways to glow

Deep-sea bioluminescence is a gift of a palette: a nearly-black blue field means every emissive color reads at full intensity. The rule here was one dark, three lights — cyan for the player and the living reef, violet for depth and bloom gradients, amber for the collectible motes (warmth = reward), and a hostile crimson reserved exclusively for danger.

Because everything renders through a bloom pass, colors were authored hotter than 1.0 in the shaders (e.g. col * 1.5) so highlights overflow into halos naturally instead of being faked with sprites.

The pairing is deliberate tension: a light italic garamond feels like something written by hand underwater — fluid, organic, slightly antique — while a tracked-out grotesk gives the HUD the cold precision of a dive instrument. The big rule: the serif is never small and the grotesk is never large. Each face does one job at one scale.

04 — THE 3D TOOLBOXNine techniques, one file

Built on Three.js with no build step — an import map, one module, custom GLSL everywhere it counts:

  • Gradient sky dome — an inverted sphere with a fragment shader blending three depths of blue, plus an animated shimmer band near the "surface" far above. It follows the camera so the world never ends.
  • Volumetric god rays — seven additive planes with a sin² falloff shader, each breathing on its own clock and swaying a few degrees. Cheap, and completely convincing in fog.
  • Exponential fog — FogExp2 is the entire depth trick. Anything far dissolves into ink, which sells scale and hides the world bounds.
  • GPU marine snow — 1,200 drifting particles whose fall and sway are computed entirely in the vertex shader from a time uniform and a per-particle seed. Zero CPU cost.
  • Ribbon tendrils — the player's trailing arms are triangle-strip ribbons rebuilt each frame from a chain of positions following each other (verlet-style "follow the leader"), tapered and faded along their length by a custom attribute. You grow a new tendril every time a cluster blooms.
  • Procedural coral — each cluster merges ~40 randomized cylinders and flattened cones into a single draw call, with per-vertex height attributes driving a sway animation and a dormant→bioluminescent color ramp in GLSL. Waking a cluster just tweens one uniform.
  • Fresnel-rimmed urchins — the enemies are merged spike geometry with a rim-light shader: near-black facing you, pulsing crimson at the silhouette. Dark-on-dark, but always legible.
  • Pooled particle bursts — one 600-slot Points pool handles every collect sparkle, sting, and bloom exhale. Nothing allocates at runtime.
  • UnrealBloomPass — the glue. One post-processing pass turns every overbright pixel into light you can almost swim through, plus CSS vignette and animated film grain on top.

05 — SOUNDAn instrument, not a soundtrack

There are no audio files. The entire soundscape is a small WebAudio synthesizer built at the moment you press DESCEND:

  • An abyssal pad — four detuned sine oscillators (A1, E2, A2, E3) through a lowpass filter whose cutoff breathes on a 16-second LFO, plus looped filtered noise for the water itself.
  • Collect chimes — a pentatonic scale that climbs as your combo grows, each note doubled by a quiet shimmer partial one octave up, fed into a feedback delay for that cathedral-underwater tail.
  • Bloom chords — an arpeggiated C–G–C–E–G swell with a deep root when a cluster wakes.
  • Urchin thud — a sine pitch-dropping from 86Hz to 34Hz. Felt more than heard.

06 — ITERATIONThree passes with a fine-toothed comb

The first build is never the shipped build. Nocturne went through three deliberate review passes, each played start-to-finish in a browser while inspecting composition, readability, and feel:

PASS ONE — COLOR & LEGIBILITY

The first screenshots were a washed teal blizzard — because the shader colors were authored in display space while the render pipeline lifts linear→sRGB at the end, brightening everything ~2×. Re-authoring every GLSL constant in linear space instantly restored the abyss. Then: snow halved and dimmed, bloom threshold raised from 0.18 to 0.38 so only genuinely bright things glow, dormant coral darkened to silhouette, urchin bodies pushed to near-black so the crimson rim does the talking.

PASS TWO — FEEL & WAYFINDING

Played start to finish and fixed what friction revealed: mote attraction radius up 40% so vacuuming a field feels like an arpeggio, spores now spawn all the way down at reef depth so the deposit run isn't empty water, coral shifted mostly behind the play plane so it can't swallow the player, ember-glow tips and brighter heart-beacons added to dormant clusters so you can find them without being told, and the awakened coral was pulled back from blown-out white so the cyan→violet ramp survives the bloom pass.

PASS THREE — DRAMA

The complexify pass: an expanding shockwave ring when coral wakes (and a small golden one on every collect, a crimson one on every sting), the title block drifting on a slow float loop, staggered letter-rise on the wordmark, celebration motes rising off woken coral forever, touch-aware copy on mobile. Small things that don't change the game but change how it feels to watch.

07 — SHIP ITDeploying to Netlify

The whole game is static files — no build step, no server — which makes deployment nearly instant:

nocturne/
├── index.html        ← the game
├── css/style.css
├── js/main.js        ← all 3D, gameplay & audio
├── guide/index.html  ← this page
└── netlify.toml
# one-time
npm i -g netlify-cli
netlify login

# from the project folder
netlify deploy --prod --dir .

That's it. Netlify serves guide/index.html at /guide automatically. Three.js loads from a CDN via an import map, fonts from Google Fonts — everything else in this project is generated the moment your browser opens it.

08 — STEAL THIS WORKFLOWThe recipe

  • Pick a world before a game. "Deep-sea bioluminescence" decided the palette, audio, typography and mechanics in one stroke.
  • One dark, few lights. Emissive colors on a near-black field + a bloom pass gives you 90% of "otherworldly" for free.
  • Author brightness above 1.0 and let post-processing make the halos.
  • Make the verbs feel good first — springy motion, magnetic pickups, sounds that climb with success.
  • Procedural everything — shaders, merged geometry and WebAudio keep the site one deploy, zero assets, infinitely tweakable.
  • Iterate like an editor, not a builder. Three passes: legibility, feel, drama — in that order.
